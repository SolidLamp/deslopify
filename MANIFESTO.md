# *The Deslopify Manifesto*
**2026-05-27**

## Preamble
The 2020s have seen the rise of artificial intelligence tools in the form of 
'Generative artificial intelligence' ('Generative AI', or 'GenAI'), which
provide generative models which produce outputs in the form of data analogous
to that which a human may produce. This includes fields such as text, software
code and images. This output data is derived from datasets of media during the
'training' (creation) of the model.

In particular, 'Large Language Models' (or 'LLMs') have seen widespread usage
for their ability to generate text and software code in ways which exhibit
behaviour comparable to that of a human, particularly by successfully
completing the 'Turing Test'.

'Generative AI' tools have been instrumental in the general replacement of
human workers, in addition to providing data centres which damage the human
environment, and otherwise providing general harm to humans. This has resulted
in a common general dislike of artificial intelligence tools.

There is a common trend, however, for these artificial intelligence tools to
see widespread usage by corporate entities, particularly on the Internet and
in software. Many users who posses a common dislike of these tools find a
diminished user experience as a result of the inclusion of these tools.

Furthermore, artificial intelligence tools often provide outputs which are
incorrect or non-factual ('hallucinations'), or not properly legally defined
in terms of their status as derivative works.

## Our Values
We believe in the ability of people to make a choice in their usage of
artificial intelligence tools. Individuals should not have artificial
intelligence tools be provided as the primary usage option for
general-purpose products, software and websites without the expressed
permission of users beforehand.

## Our Beliefs
We believe that output from 'Generative AI'/'Large Language Model' systems
has been derived from the copyrighted materials of all individuals' work
included within its dataset.

In artificial intelligence systems where these datasets were derived from
publicly-available data on the Internet (such as that of open-source
software), it is often not possible to provide any formal agreement from the
original copyright owners, particularly when the dataset included non-libre
original works or works that otherwise prohibit or place restrictions on
distribution.

It can thus be concluded that the distributor of AI-generated content has no
legitimate claim to the copyright of such material, and thus has no legitimate
ability to place such materials under an open-source licence or otherwise
publicly distribute it.


## Our Aims
We aim to provide users with the choice on their usage of 'Generative AI'
tools by providing users with default configurations which
exclude these 'Generative AI' tools, and providing users with the option to
enable to disable these features, without such tools being provided as the
default option.

We aim to develop our software without any artificial intelligence tools
involved in the development process.

We aim to provide our software under free, open-source licences that enable
all individuals to utilize our software.
